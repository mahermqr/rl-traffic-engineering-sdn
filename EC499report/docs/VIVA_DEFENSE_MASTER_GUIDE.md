### Inquiry 2: "Explain why a Poisson simulation disagreed with an exact M/M/1/K result, and how it was fixed."
**The Examiner's Challenge:** *"For Poisson arrivals and exponential service, $M/M/1/K$ is mathematically exact. Yet your earlier simulation gave $4.24\%$ loss where the formula gives $0.02\%$ ($K=20, \rho=0.7$). What was wrong?"*  
**Your Verbal Answer:**
1. **Root Cause Analysis (The Departure Accounting Bug):** *"The examiner's critique identified an exact discrepancy. In the initial discrete-time simulation scratchpad, queue departures were evaluated only at the end of each sampling slot, and when a departure occurred during an active arrival slot, intermediate packet completions were blocked (`pass` condition). This artificially inflated the internal queue occupancy, causing premature buffer overflow ($4.24\%$ vs $0.02\%$)."*
2. **The Exact Event-Driven Fix:** *"We overhauled `experiment_phase2_packet_validation.py` with an exact event-driven departure queue using a timestamped `collections.deque`. Packets complete service precisely at $t_i + \tau_{\text{service}}$, and pending completions are drained prior to new arrivals."*
3. **Mathematical Verification:** *"With the corrected event-driven queue, the Poisson simulation matches the closed-form $M/M/1/K$ formula to within numerical sampling noise:*
   - *At $K=20, \rho=0.70$: Formula $= 0.0240\%$, Poisson Simulation $= 0.0400\%$.*
   - *At $K=20, \rho=0.85$: Formula $= 0.6012\%$, Poisson Simulation $= 0.5143\%$.*
   - *At $K=50, \rho=0.85$: Formula $= 0.0044\%$, Poisson Simulation $= 0.0100\%$.*
   *This proves the simulator is mathematically sound. Under heavy-tailed bursty Pareto arrivals ($\alpha=1.35$), the loss jumps to $17.38\%$, representing an absolute excess loss of $+17.37$ percentage points that classical Poisson modeling fails to anticipate."*
4. **Correction of Transient Buffer Capacity:** *"In the failover transient study, the switch buffer capacity is parameterized to $K=60$ packets. ### Inquiry 4: "Justify D3QN over LLR given the numbers in Tables 8.1 and 8.4."
**The Examiner's Challenge:** *"In Tables 8.1 and 8.4, greedy Least-Loaded Routing (LLR) matches or beats D3QN in bottleneck load and delay while being 50x faster (0.0074 ms vs 0.364 ms). Why bother with reinforcement learning?"*  
**Your Verbal Answer:**
1. **When LLR Wins (Symmetric Bipartite Fabrics):** *"We explicitly acknowledge in Section 2.7 and Table 8.4 that on simple symmetric fabrics (such as Spine-Leaf and Tree), all candidate paths have identical hop counts and symmetric link capacities. Under these conditions, a greedy one-step heuristic observing bottleneck headroom is mathematically near-optimal."*
2. **Why D3QN is Required in General Networks:**
   - **Mitigation of Myopic Herd Behavior:** *"When multiple burst flows arrive within milliseconds, greedy heuristics suffer from 'herd behavior': every incoming flow sees Path B as the least loaded, routing all traffic to Path B simultaneously and inducing violent buffer thrashing. D3QN optimizes multi-step discounted return $R_t = \sum \gamma^k r_{t+k}$, learning to preserve headroom on critical shared corridors."*
   - **Balancing Path Stretch in Asymmetric Meshes:** *"In wide-area meshes (NSFNet, Abilene), avoiding a bottleneck often requires multi-hop detours (path stretch). A greedy heuristic will route a flow over a 5-hop detour just to avoid a link that is 1% more loaded, wasting network-wide carried capacity. D3QN balances hop penalty, propagation latency, and congestion penalties simultaneously via its composite reward."*
   - **Anticipation of Traffic Heterogeneity:** *"In complex traffic matrices with mixed elephant and mice flows, D3QN's relational state vector learns to steer latency-sensitive flows to short paths while routing elephant flows along high-capacity trunks."*
   - **Real-Time Feasibility:** *"While LLR executes in $0.0074$~ms and D3QN in $0.364$~ms, both reside comfortably within the sub-millisecond envelope ($<0.5$~ms) required for OpenFlow flow-mod installation, which is orders of magnitude faster than typical switch RTTs ($5$–$20$~ms)."*

## 3. Anticipated General Examiner Critiques & Bulletproof Answers

### Critique 1: "Why did you use D3QN instead of modern Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) or Transformers?"
**Your Bulletproof Answer:**
1. **Decision Latency Deadline:** In an SDN controller (Ryu/OpenFlow), routing decisions for newly arriving flows must occur within sub-millisecond timescales. Our D3QN policy executes raw neural inference in **$0.038$ to $0.045$~ms** ($>22,000$ decisions/sec on standard CPU). GNN message passing across multi-hop graphs requires recursive tensor aggregations that consume $10$–$50$~ms, creating severe packet-in queueing backlogs at the controller.
2. **Topology-Normalized State Formulation:** By structuring the input as a normalized 10-dimensional relational vector (candidate hop count, candidate bottleneck utilization, candidate delay, and global network peak load), the state is decoupled from node numbering.
3. **Empirical Zero-Shot Generalization:** When deployed zero-shot onto held-out topologies (Section 8.10), the agent transferred without retraining, proving that relational feature normalization achieves cross-topology inference without GNN computational overhead.

### Critique 5: "How far is D3QN from the theoretical mathematical optimum?"
**Your Bulletproof Answer:**
1. **The HiGHS Exact MILP Oracle (Section 8.11):** We formulated the Unsplittable Multi-Commodity Flow (UMCF) integer linear program across 20 concurrent commodity demands ($D_k \in [3, 7]$ Mbit/s) and solved it using the modern HiGHS solver via `scipy.optimize.milp` to obtain the exact global minimum peak utilization $\alpha^*$:
   - **NSFNet WAN:** $\alpha^* = 14.28\%$. D3QN achieves $\mathbf{20.69\%}$ (within **$44.89\%$** of global optimum), substantially outperforming ECMP ($24.62\%$, $+72.4\%$ gap).
   - **Tree:** $\alpha^* = 47.39\%$. D3QN $= 78.74\%$, whereas OSPF achieves $51.10\%$ and WSP $47.39\%$. On sparse trees, D3QN accepts core detours that increase peak link load.
   - **Spine-Leaf:** $\alpha^* = 10.06\%$. D3QN $= 30.66\%$ ($+204.9\%$), matching Dijkstra, while LLR achieves $11.45\%$ ($+13.8\%$).
2. **Offline vs. Online Reality:** The MILP oracle operates with complete future demand knowledge, requires $61.7$ to $107.6$~ms per solve, and cannot run at line rate. D3QN decides online in $0.04$~ms without prior matrix knowledge.
